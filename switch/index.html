A switch you build instead of buy

Open Adaptive Switch is an open source Bluetooth button for the iOS Switch Control accessibility feature. The parts come to about $15; comparable commercial switches sell for around $200. Press it and it sends a keystroke over Bluetooth, which an iPhone or iPad treats as an accessibility switch input.

Build guide on GitHub Web setup page

The companion iPhone app is on its way to the App Store. Until it arrives, the web setup page does the same job from a browser.

One button, set up from a phone

Each unit is a single button in a box: a Seeed XIAO nRF52840 board, a momentary button, and a small rechargeable battery, charged over USB-C. A family can configure everything over Bluetooth with no computer and no reflashing: which key the button sends, one action or three from different press lengths, how long before it sleeps, its name, and its color. Firmware updates arrive the same way. Settings live on the switch itself and survive restarts and updates.

The battery lasts months per charge, because the board holds its Bluetooth connection at roughly 0.1mA. The switch shows its battery level in the app, on the setup page, and in the iOS Batteries widget.

Made for iOS Switch Control

The switch shows up to iOS as a Bluetooth keyboard and pairs in Settings. With Switch Control, a press can tap the screen, run a recipe, or drive the scanner. Each key registers as its own switch input, so three action mode behaves like three switches in one box. It also works outside Switch Control: set it to Space and it starts and pauses YouTube or Apple Music, and the Stories app listens for its key presses directly, with no Switch Control setup at all.

The switch in detail

The switch has its own site at openadaptiveswitch.com, with the full feature tour, support and troubleshooting, and the privacy policy for the companion app. The repository holds the build guide, firmware, releases, and the documented Bluetooth protocol.

Build one

Parts list, wiring, firmware, and the app source are all in one repository. Assembly is wiring a button between two pins and plugging in a battery.
